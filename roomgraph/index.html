ROOMGRAPH / SYNTHETIC INDOOR STRUCTURE

Learning the shape of a room.

In development · First dataset milestone complete

RoomGraph explores how multiple indoor images can reveal a building’s 3D structure. The first step is a synthetic dataset with precise architectural edge labels: wall intersections, floor and ceiling boundaries, and door and window openings.

The working prototype uses NVIDIA Isaac Sim on a DGX Spark to render a room and export calibrated images, metric depth, normals, and structural-edge ground truth. The next stages are learned edge detection, multi-view reconstruction, and furnished environments.

What is validated

The native ARM64 installation renders all four views successfully. Independent ray–geometry checks verify the camera and depth conventions, with a 95th-percentile depth difference below 0.003 mm in each view.

This agreement is a synthetic calibration check, not measured reconstruction accuracy. Five unit tests cover projection, ray intersections, openings, invalid poses, and occlusion.

What comes next

  • Procedural connected rooms and larger layouts.
  • An edge/corner model, training pipeline, and held-out evaluation.
  • Multi-view reconstruction, first with known camera poses.
  • Furnished kitchens, offices, bedrooms, garages, and factories.

Dense-scene throughput and real-image generalization are still to be evaluated. The current demo uses an empty room with opaque surfaces and an unglazed window opening.
